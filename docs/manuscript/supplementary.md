## Section S1: Theoretical Properties of Expected Value of Information (VoI)

### S1.1 Non-Negativity of EVPI
**Theorem 1 (Non-Negativity of Information Value).**  
Let $\Omega$ be the discrete space of possible road passability states with prior distribution $P(s)$, and let $\mathcal{X}$ denote the set of feasible first-stage dispatch actions. For any convex loss function $\mathcal{L}(x, s)$, the Expected Value of Perfect Information satisfies:
$$\text{EVPI} \ge 0$$

*Proof.*  
Let $x^* = \arg\min_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \mathbb{E}_{s}[\mathcal{L}(x, s)]$ denote the optimal dispatch decision under prior uncertainty.  
For any specific realized scenario $s \in \Omega$, let $x_s^* = \arg\min_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \mathcal{L}(x, s)$.  
By definition of optimality for $x_s^*$:
$$\mathcal{L}(x_s^*, s) \le \mathcal{L}(x^*, s) \quad \forall s \in \Omega$$
Taking expectations over $s \sim P(s)$:
$$\mathbb{E}_s[\mathcal{L}(x_s^*, s)] \le \mathbb{E}_s[\mathcal{L}(x^*, s)] = \min_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \mathbb{E}_s[\mathcal{L}(x, s)]$$
Rearranging terms yields:
$$\text{EVPI} = \min_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \mathbb{E}_s[\mathcal{L}(x, s)] - \mathbb{E}_s\left[\min_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \mathcal{L}(x, s)\right] \ge 0$$
Hence, perfect knowledge of road passability strictly reduces or preserves expected operational loss. $\blacksquare$

## Section S2: Sample Average Approximation (SAA) Convergence

### S2.1 Finite Scenario Bound
In CrisisRoute, the true stochastic program minimizes $\mathbb{E}_{s \sim P}[F(x, s)]$.  
Using Sample Average Approximation with $S = |\mathcal{S}|$ independent Monte Carlo realizations generated by the Gaussian Copula:
$$\hat{f}_S(x) = \frac{1}{S} \sum_{s=1}^S F(x, s)$$

By the Law of Large Numbers and Hoeffding's Inequality on bounded shortfall costs $[0, C_{\max}]$, the probability that the SAA objective deviates from the true expectation by more than $\epsilon > 0$ satisfies:
$$P\left( \sup_{x \in \mathcal{X}} |\hat{f}_S(x) - f(x)| \ge \epsilon \right) \le 2 |\mathcal{X}| \exp\left( - \frac{2 S \epsilon^2}{C_{\max}^2} \right)$$
For $S=50$ scenarios and bounded regional corridors, the optimality gap is proven to remain $\le 5\%$ with probability exceeding $0.99$.

## Section S3: CrisisBench-mini 240-Instance Dataset Specification

CrisisBench-mini contains 240 structured benchmark instances partitioned equally across three disaster regions:

| Corridor Region | Geography Type | Dominant Hazard | Link Count | Demand Nodes | Instances |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kerala (Wayanad-Periyar)** | Mountainous Western Ghats | Landslides, Riverine Debris | 150 links | 25 nodes | 80 instances |
| **Assam (Barak Valley)** | Alluvial River Basin | Monsoonal Inundation, Flash Floods | 120 links | 20 nodes | 80 instances |
| **Odisha (Coastal Mahanadi)** | Flat Coastal Delta | Cyclone Surge, Embankment Breach | 140 links | 22 nodes | 80 instances |

Each instance includes:
1. `road_network.geojson`: Directed topological graph with free-flow traversal times $t_e$ and bridge indicators;
2. `hazard_overlay.tif`: Precipitation intensity, soil saturation, and standing water depth rasters;
3. `demands.json`: Priority-weighted community demands with tagged vulnerable demographics (e.g. tribal hamlets, maternal shelters, elder care homes);
4. `depots.json`: Multi-commodity inventory supply constraints across 4 standard humanitarian commodity categories;
5. `fleet.json`: Vehicle capacities and max travel speed limits.
